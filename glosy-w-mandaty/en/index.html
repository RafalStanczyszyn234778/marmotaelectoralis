Step 2 · 41 districts

Every vote counts separately in each district

There's no national pool. Each district divides its own seats.

Once we know who made it into parliament, we can pretty much set the national result aside. From now on only the districts matter…

Why 20 seats in Warsaw but 8 in Wałbrzych? - expand switch the map view

A district's number of MPs is calculated by dividing its population by a single representation quota and rounding - currently - residents per seat. The catch: the current seat numbers were set in 2001 and carried over into the 2011 Electoral Code.

Step 3 · D'Hondt - is it that scary?

We divide until the seats run out

The whole D'Hondt method fits in one sentence. We divide each list's votes by 1, 2, 3, 4… - as many times as there are seats, and the largest number in the table takes a seat.

    Step 4 · who exactly gets in

    The list won seats. But who becomes an MP?

    A seat belongs to the list, not to a person. If a list wins three seats in a district, they go to the three candidates with the most votes - the order on the ballot has nothing to do with it, only votes.
